Four modelling decisions the cases forced

Country in the title

For generic bodies, identity includes jurisdiction.

Unlinked as an outcome

A mention may stay a mention when the source cannot settle it.

Provenance on each link

iwac:nerModel, iwac:descriptionModel: which model proposed this value.

Undated descriptions

Written today about articles decades old. Patched by a prompt rule; IWAC lacks a temporal marker.

Questions for the Alevi Archive

  1. Where does the identity of a person or a cemaat live, and who decides that two factoids are about the same entity?
  2. Can a probabilistic identification stay marked as such once it is in the graph?
  3. Is a check by the same model a verification at all? What would count as an independent one?

Slide 26

How step 2 matches names

Code, no model. Looser keys are tried only once every exact key has missed.

  • Exact keys: lower case, no accents, spaces or dashes
  • A key shared by two records is ambiguous, never resolved
  • Word order: “Coulibaly Kassoum” = “Kassoum Coulibaly”; never for initials
  • Plural fold for non-person subjects; accents kept, “Jeunes” ≠ “Jeûne”
  • Acronyms of 2–6 capitals get a case-sensitive key only
  • “(Country)” titles are also keyed bare within that country’s run

Further questions

  1. Should provenance sit on every link, and what does that cost in a factoid model where links are the data?
  2. How do you tell apart two people, or two ocaks, of one name in different regions? Is that decision a factoid, with a source and an author?
