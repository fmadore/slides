Slide 3

What an MCP server does

The Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard since late 2024, lets an AI assistant use tools that another organisation publishes.

1 A person asks

In Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant: “What does AMIRA hold on highlife?”

2 The assistant calls AMIRA’s tools

Search, count, read a record. It can only use the tools the server offers.

3 The server answers from the catalogue

Records, counts and passages, each with a link to its page in AMIRA.

4 The assistant writes the answer

In its own words, ideally citing the records it received.

Who decides what We decide which tools exist and what they return. The assistant and its provider decide what reaches the answer.

Two tools this pilot builds on

Linking keywords to Wikidata
  • CAREL, led by Jiayu Yang: linking without task-specific training
  • Rules settle the clear cases; a local open-source model, the ambiguous ones
  • 89.8% and 90.9% accuracy on Library of Congress headings

Yang et al., JCDL 2026, doi:10.1145/3805696.3846031; CIKM 2026, doi:10.1145/3799682.3840034

AMIRA MCP server
  • AI assistants query AMIRA through read-only tools
  • The server, not the model, computes counts, facets and related records
  • Every result links to its record
  • A curator mode also serves private full text

Slide 9

Highlife, defined from AMIRA’s sources

Highlife is a popular dance music of urban West Africa, above all Ghana and Nigeria, that fuses African and Western musical styles. Writers describe it as a compromise between African traditional forms and Western music, mostly sung in many vernaculars with simple, binary-form melodies. It emerged as a national rather than ethnic music in the 1950s.

The same models without AMIRA’s sources

Model alone (route C)
  • All four: a fluent definition of 51 to 96 words, in under ten seconds
  • A common account: Ghana, early 1900s, local rhythms, Western brass
  • No source to check it against
Reading AMIRA (route D): Claude Haiku 5.5
  • Reports what the journal’s authors say
  • Konkomba singing bands, the vernaculars, weekend gatherings, a debated origin
  • Each claim cites a quote that code checks against the text read
Not an accuracy result On highlife, and again on the lesser-known timbila, the models alone stayed fluent and plausible. The difference shown is one of traceability and emphasis. One run per model per route.

Slide 13

Three limits of the pilot

Older articles use older vocabulary
6 to 7uses of “tribal” per 10,000 words, 1957 and 1962

Under 1 from 2007 to 2023, apart from one sparse year, in the journal African Music. A definition needs the dates of its sources.

Most mentions are in passing
0 of 46articles that mention “timbila” carry a heading for it

31 of the 46 mention it once or twice. “Mbira” occurs in 219 full texts; 18 records carry the heading “Mbira music”.

The code checks quotes only
7 of 7claims with a quote found in the text

It confirms that each quote appears in the text the model read. Whether the claim is right needs a reader who knows the field.

“Tribal”: AMIRA MCP server, full-text map of 657 African Music articles, 16 November 2026.

Slide 14

What comes next in the pilot

1 Repeated runs

Several runs per model and a calibrated judge model, before anything counts as a measurement.

2 A local model

Ministral 3, to reduce reliance on the services of major US technology companies. Hardware and maintenance capacity differ across partner institutions.

3 More concepts

Including ones where the evidence is insufficient.

4 Review by researchers

Contributing researchers and subject specialists assess and revise the drafts.
