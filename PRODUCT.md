# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: academic audiences — digital humanities, African and Islamic studies,
history — watching a talk projected in a lecture hall at a conference, seminar
or keynote. When a design trade-off pits the hall against any later reader,
**the hall wins** (confirmed 2026-08-17).

Secondary: readers who later browse the published archive at
slides.frederickmadore.com or its exported PDFs — colleagues following up,
people citing a deck.

Operator: Frédérick Madore, historian of Islam in francophone West Africa and
data curator at the Africa Multiple Cluster of Excellence (University of
Bayreuth). He authors, presents and maintains every deck himself.

## Product Purpose

A personal collection of conference talks, lectures and keynotes, published as
offline-capable reveal.js decks on one shared engine and theme. Success
(confirmed 2026-08-17): each deck (1) reads as an authored scholarly artefact
that builds the presenter's credibility as a historian and digital humanist,
and (2) stands as a durable, citable research output (stable URLs, per-deck
PDFs, CITATION.cff). Links/QR to IWAC and the personal site, and in-deck
method demos, occur but are not the primary success measures.

## Positioning

An authored scholarly broadsheet, not a slide template: decks a template
service could not truthfully produce. Fully self-hosted and offline-capable
(vendored fonts, reveal.js and highlight.js — no CDNs), one shared engine so
every published talk benefits from every fix, validated and exported by its
own CI. The author is the credit; the university is an affiliation.

## Operating Context

- Presented live from a laptop in lecture halls on projectors of unpredictable
  quality; must stay legible at distance and survive weak beamers. The
  presenter relies on speaker notes (S), the TOC overlay (T), fragments, and
  mid-talk blackout (B).
- Decks are served over HTTP; local development via `serve-deck.py`
  (no-cache, http://localhost:8742).
- Published by GitHub Actions to GitHub Pages at slides.frederickmadore.com.
  The validation gate: Python/Node unit tests, `tools/audit.py --strict`,
  Playwright checks at 1280×720 / 844×390 / 390×844; PRs touching the shared
  engine additionally get screenshot visual regression. The deploy strips
  speaker notes and generates per-deck `slides.pdf` + `social-card.png`.
- Some slides embed live iframes (`.site-frame`, needs network, degrades to a
  labelled placeholder) and vendored file embeds (`.scroll-panel`, offline).
- Decks are published in English and French (per-talk `lang` in
  `talks/talks.json`); typography must carry French diacritics well.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Fixed 1280×720 reveal.js canvas, uniformly scaled to fit any screen —
  slides shrink, never reflow. The type scale is deliberately fixed `rem`,
  not viewport-fluid; only chrome outside the canvas (footer, TOC overlay)
  needs small-screen handling.
- Authoring is plain HTML `<section>`s only. The Markdown plugin was removed
  deliberately — never reintroduce `data-markdown`.
- Everything self-hosted/offline: fonts re-vendored via
  `tools/fetch-fonts.py`, reveal.js v6 vendored, slim highlight.js via
  `tools/fetch-highlight.py`, checksums in `shared/vendor-manifest.json`.
- One shared engine: `shared/src/` (theme + deck partials) builds into the
  stable public bundles `shared/theme.css` + `shared/deck.js` via
  `npm run build:shared` (`npm test` fails if stale). Every published talk
  consumes the same bundles, so engine changes ripple into delivered
  archives — published decks are never individually redesigned.
- Talk scaffolding, the manifest (`talks/talks.json`), landing page, sitemap
  and QR codes are generated by `tools/` scripts; SEO and DECK_CONFIG blocks
  are generated from the manifest — edit outside the marked blocks.
- Auto-fit engine with `?check` overlay; a fit scale below 0.90 fails
  validation unless excused with `data-fit-allow="reason"`.
- Licence split: MIT (engine, tooling) + CC BY 4.0 (slide text, original
  figures); vendored software, fonts, logos and external images excluded.
- The source repo is public: speaker notes are stripped from the live site
  but remain readable on GitHub — nothing confidential belongs in notes.

## Product Principles

1. The hall is the primary reader: legibility at projection distance outranks
   screen-reading niceties whenever the two conflict.
2. Every deck is a citable scholarly artefact: stable URLs, faithful PDFs and
   correct attribution are product features, not afterthoughts.
3. One engine, many talks: improvements land in the shared engine so the
   whole archive benefits; published decks are never redesigned one by one.
4. Self-contained or absent: anything a deck needs at show time ships in the
   repo; the few live-network elements must degrade gracefully.
5. Authored, not generated: the presenter's editorial judgment must be
   visible; nothing that reads as boilerplate survives review.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the binding bar for the published site (confirmed 2026-08-17):
contrast on the colour fields, visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion`
honoured (the motion signature already stills under it), alt/title enforced by
`tools/audit.py`. Projection legibility — distance and weak beamers — is an
additional product-specific requirement on top of AA, not a substitute for it.
